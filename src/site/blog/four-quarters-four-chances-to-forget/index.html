Four quarters, four chances to forget.

A rotating card pays its 5 percent only after you tell the issuer you want it. Here's why that step keeps getting skipped, and the habit that makes it stop.

$0 of $1,500 this quarter

A rotating category card is the only card in your wallet that comes with homework.

Every other card does what it says on the tile. The grocery card earns at supermarkets. The flat card earns 2 percent on everything and asks nothing of you. The rotating card earns 5 percent in a set of categories that changes four times a year, on up to $1,500 of spend a quarter, and only once you've gone into your issuer's app and pressed a button that says, in effect, yes, I'd like the money.

Miss the button and the card earns its base rate, usually 1 percent, in exactly the categories it was supposed to earn 5 percent in. Nothing tells you. The statement arrives looking normal.

01Why the activation step exists at all

Issuers don't add a step by accident. Activation is a filter: a cardholder who forgets costs the issuer nothing that quarter, and every quarter some do. The step has worked that way for years.

That isn't a scandal. It's the deal on the tile, read closely. But it does mean the card's rate is not really 5 percent. It's 5 percent multiplied by the fraction of quarters you remember. Two of four and you own a card that pays 2.5 percent in its bonus categories, with a cap.

It's 5 percent multiplied by the fraction of quarters you remember.

02Where the quarter actually goes wrong

Ask anyone who has missed a quarter and the story is the same. Nothing happened. The quarter opened on a Wednesday. There was an email, somewhere under other emails. Life didn't mention it. In week seven they stood at a register in a department store that was, that quarter, a bonus category, and tapped a different card, because the rotating one hadn't been activated and anyway they couldn't remember what the categories were.

The failure isn't carelessness. It's that the quarter is an event on the issuer's calendar and nowhere on yours. There's no natural moment in a normal life that says: it's the first day of a new quarter, open the app.

03What four of four looks like

Cash Pass carries one example year through every screen, so here is the rotating card's line in it. Four quarters, all four activated. $3,600 of spend landed in bonus categories across the year, spread so no quarter passed the $1,500 cap. At 5 percent that's $180. The card has no annual fee, so the $180 is the whole story.

Now the version with two quarters missed, which is closer to how these cards are actually used. Half of that $3,600 earns 1 percent instead of 5, and the card's year shrinks by more than a third. The shrinking is invisible, because nothing ever said the money was there in the first place.

Example figures, and your issuer sets and pays the rewards. But the shape holds for any rotating card: the difference between the good year and the ordinary one is not spending. It's remembering.

04The habit that holds

Habits that survive are the ones attached to a cue that already exists. The quarter has no cue, so give it one. Three parts.

First, a reminder that arrives before the quarter opens, not after. A message a few days ahead that names the categories does the work of the email you never found. Pass sends that reminder before every quarter, and it keeps nudging while the card sits unactivated.

Second, a mark. When you've pressed the issuer's button, record it. The Quarter screen in Cash Pass has one button, Mark activated, and a line under it that reads like a record: Activated: Q1, Q2, Q3. Until that mark exists, Cash Pass treats the card as earning its base rate and won't recommend it for the bonus, so an unactivated card can't quietly win a till.

Third, a bar. $0 of $1,500 this quarter, then $900 of $1,500, then the cap. Watching the number move is what turns a chore into a small game you're already winning.

05What Cash Pass does, and what your issuer does

The split is exact. Cash Pass reminds, tracks and records. Your issuer activates, sets the categories, sets the cap and pays the reward. Cash Pass has no button that activates anything on your account, because its connection to your cards is read only. It can see the spend. It can't touch the card.

That's the right shape for this. The forgetting was never about power. It was about the calendar. The fix is a calendar that knows the quarter and a wallet that knows whether you've done the one thing.
